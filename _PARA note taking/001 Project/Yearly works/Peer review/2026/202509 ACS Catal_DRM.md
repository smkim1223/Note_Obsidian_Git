## Claude summary
### Peer Review Comments

**Manuscript:** Wang et al. "Improved Coking Resistance and Thermal Stability of Pd/Al2O3 Catalyst by Atomic Layer Deposition of Iron Oxide for Dry Reforming of Methane"

**Recommendation:** Reject - Major revisions required before consideration at ACS Catalysis

### Overall Assessment

While the concept of using ALD Fe2O3 coatings to enhance Pd/Al2O3 catalyst performance for dry reforming of methane (DRM) is scientifically interesting, this manuscript contains several fundamental experimental and analytical flaws that significantly compromise the reliability and validity of the conclusions. The work requires substantial additional experimentation and analysis before it meets the standards expected for publication in ACS Catalysis.

### Major Concerns

### 1. Critical Experimental Design Flaws

**Catalyst Activation Protocol:** The authors provide no clear description of catalyst reduction/activation procedures prior to DRM testing. Given that the catalyst is calcined at 500°C in air (forming PdO), the absence of a reduction step raises serious questions about catalyst activation and the actual active phase during DRM. This is a fundamental oversight that undermines the entire study.

**Inadequate Quantitative Analysis:** The conversion and selectivity calculations are not properly explained. The authors fail to account for volume expansion during DRM (CH4 + CO2 → 2CO + 2H2) and do not use internal standards (e.g., N2) for quantitative MS analysis. This makes all reported conversion and selectivity data questionable.

### 2. Insufficient Characterization and Experimental Justification

**ALD Parameter Selection:** The choice of exactly 20 ALD cycles lacks proper justification. No systematic study of Fe2O3 thickness effects on catalytic performance is provided, which is essential for understanding structure-activity relationships.

**Textural Property Analysis:** The manuscript shows insufficient characterization of porosity changes. Surface area decreases from 130 to 100 m²/g, yet the authors claim "no pore blockage" without adequate supporting evidence. N2 physisorption data for porosity creation after 900°C treatment is missing.

**Morphological Characterization Gaps:**

- Particle size distributions are not compared before/after ALD deposition and DRM reaction
- HR-TEM evidence for claimed "conformal Fe2O3 layer" is lacking
- The TEM images show well-dispersed Fe but do not conclusively demonstrate conformal layer deposition

### 3. Mechanistic Understanding Deficiencies

**Oxygen Availability Claims:** The authors attribute enhanced performance to Fe2O3 providing lattice oxygen, but fail to distinguish between PdO and Fe2O3 reduction contributions. A benchmark Fe2O3/Al2O3 catalyst comparison is needed to isolate Fe2O3 effects.

**Temperature-Programmed Analysis Issues:** The CO-TPR experiments raise concerns about selectivity - how do the authors ensure CO2 formation is specifically from PdO reduction rather than Fe2O3 reduction?

**Coking Mechanism Misrepresentation:** The coking experiments use 5% CH4/N2 at 900°C, which represents methane decomposition rather than DRM coking conditions. This does not properly assess coking resistance under actual DRM conditions.

### 4. Inadequate Supporting Calculations and Analysis

**Thickness Calculations:** The Fe2O3 thickness calculation (~0.4 nm for 20 cycles) lacks detailed mathematical derivation and supporting equations. Given the complex Al2O3 phase structure after 900°C calcination, uniform thickness assumptions are questionable.

**Activation Energy Analysis:** While activation energy trends are reported, the discussion lacks depth regarding how different Fe2O3 deposition methods (ALD vs. IWI) fundamentally alter the temperature dependence of DRM kinetics.

### 5. Experimental Condition Mischaracterization

**"Severe Conditions" Claim:** The authors claim CH4-rich conditions represent "severe" DRM conditions, but this is debatable. Severe DRM conditions typically involve high concentrations of both reactants or lower temperatures where both Boudouard reaction and methane decomposition are favored.

**Surface Analysis Limitations:** XPS analysis of coked catalysts may not provide reliable Fe signal changes due to limited penetration depth through carbon deposits, questioning conclusions about Fe2O3 as a "sacrificial layer."

### Minor Issues

- Phase characterization of Al2O3 support before and after ALD deposition is needed given the complex phase transformations at 900°C
- Reference to relevant literature on Fe-based catalysts for coke mitigation could be expanded
- Statistical analysis and error bars for key performance metrics are missing

### Recommendation

This manuscript requires major revisions addressing fundamental experimental design flaws, particularly catalyst activation protocols, quantitative analysis methodology, and comprehensive characterization before it can be considered suitable for publication in ACS Catalysis. The current work, while addressing an interesting topic, falls short of the rigorous experimental standards expected for this premier catalysis journal.

I recommend the authors:

1. Conduct proper catalyst reduction studies and clearly document activation procedures
2. Implement rigorous quantitative analysis with internal standards
3. Perform systematic ALD cycle optimization studies
4. Provide comprehensive textural and morphological characterization
5. Include appropriate control experiments (Fe2O3/Al2O3 benchmark)
6. Conduct DRM coking studies under actual reaction conditions

Given the extent of required revisions, I suggest this work would benefit from submission to a journal more suitable for preliminary catalytic studies after addressing these fundamental issues.


## Claude point-by-point summary
### Peer Review Comments

**Manuscript:** Wang et al. "Improved Coking Resistance and Thermal Stability of Pd/Al2O3 Catalyst by Atomic Layer Deposition of Iron Oxide for Dry Reforming of Methane"

**Recommendation:** Reject - Major revisions required before consideration at ACS Catalysis

### Overall Assessment

While the concept of using ALD Fe2O3 coatings to enhance Pd/Al2O3 catalyst performance for dry reforming of methane (DRM) is scientifically interesting, this manuscript contains several fundamental experimental and analytical flaws that significantly compromise the reliability and validity of the conclusions. The work requires substantial additional experimentation and analysis before it meets the standards expected for publication in ACS Catalysis.

### Specific Point-by-Point Comments

### 1. Catalyst Preparation and Activation (p.5)

**Issue:** The authors didn't account for reduction prior to DRM test, i.e., PdO/Al2O3. It questions how the author can activate the catalyst for DRM.

**Comment:** After calcination at 500°C in air for 6 hours to remove organics and nitrates, the Pd would be present as PdO. However, no reduction step is described before DRM testing. This is a critical omission as the activation protocol directly affects the active phase and catalytic performance. The authors must provide detailed catalyst activation procedures.

### 2. ALD Process Optimization (p.6)

**Issue:** The authors showed only 20 cycles ALD of Fe2O3. It may question how the thickness of Fe2O3 layer influences DRM activity and stability, and why the specific thickness of Fe2O3 cycles the authors considered in this work.

**Comment:** The selection of exactly 20 ALD cycles appears arbitrary without systematic optimization. A study examining different numbers of ALD cycles (e.g., 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 cycles) is essential to understand the structure-activity relationship and determine the optimal Fe2O3 thickness for DRM performance.

### 3. Control Catalyst Characterization (p.6)

**Issue:** The deposition of Fe2O3 by IWI would be interesting compared to ALD deposition. But, the authors may consider to show how Fe deposited on Pd/Al2O3 (a kind of layer) and it has been transformed into particles either during drying or calcination.

**Comment:** The morphological evolution of IWI-deposited Fe2O3 during preparation steps needs characterization. Understanding whether IWI forms layers or particles compared to ALD is crucial for meaningful comparison between the two deposition methods.

### 4. Temperature-Programmed Reduction Analysis (p.7)

**Issue:** How does the author ensure CO2 can be selectively formed from PdO, not likely Fe2O3?

**Comment:** During CO-TPR experiments, the authors monitor CO2 formation but do not distinguish between CO2 from PdO reduction versus Fe2O3 reduction. This ambiguity undermines claims about PdO-Fe2O3 interactions. Control experiments with Fe2O3/Al2O3 are needed to isolate contributions.

### 5. Quantitative Analysis Methodology (p.7)

**Issue:** It is concerned how the authors quantify and analyze the conversion and production formation. By considering volume expansion (CH4 + CO2 → 2CO + 2H2), tracking the internal standard of N2 can be required. So, the authors are required to explicitly explain the conversion and selectivity calculation.

**Comment:** The manuscript lacks proper quantitative analysis methodology. DRM involves volume expansion, yet no internal standard (N2) is used for MS quantification. The authors must provide detailed equations for conversion and selectivity calculations and validate their quantitative approach.

### 6. Coking Test Experimental Design (p.7)

**Issue:** This is the coke formation by methane decomposition, not from DRM. Fe2O3 can mitigate coke formation by facilitating CO2 on the surface.

**Comment:** The coking experiments using 5% CH4/N2 at 900°C represent methane decomposition rather than DRM coking conditions. This does not properly assess coking resistance under actual DRM conditions where both CH4 and CO2 are present. The authors should conduct coking studies under realistic DRM feed compositions.

### 7. Support Characterization (p.8)

**Issue:** The authors may consider discussing the textural properties about the porosity changes with Pd/Al2O3. The authors calcined Al2O3 at 900°C, which results in multi-phases of Al2O3. Therefore, it is required to characterize it before and after ALD Fe2O3 deposition.

**Comment:** Calcination at 900°C creates multiple Al2O3 phases with varying surface properties. Phase identification and textural characterization before and after ALD deposition are essential to understand the ALD growth mechanism and its impact on catalyst properties.

### 8. Thickness Calculation Methodology (p.8)

**Issue:** The authors need to provide more details with the equation.

**Comment:** The calculation of Fe2O3 thickness (~0.4 nm) lacks mathematical details. Given the assumption of uniform coverage over complex Al2O3 phases, detailed equations and underlying assumptions must be provided and validated.

### 9. ALD Growth Uniformity (p.8)

**Issue:** By given statement a mixture of Al2O3 phases, it questions how the authors can provide the corresponding thickness with mass and the surface area of Pd/Al2O3. Any supporting evidence of HR-TEM or IR upon the cycles can be considered.

**Comment:** The presence of multiple Al2O3 phases with different -OH densities challenges uniform ALD growth assumptions. HR-TEM or IR spectroscopy evidence across different ALD cycles would strengthen claims about conformal deposition.

### 10. Surface Area Changes (p.9)

**Issue:** Surface area was changed from 130 m2/g to 100 m2/g. The authors need to discuss in more detail how they exclude pore blockage.

**Comment:** The significant surface area decrease (130 to 100 m²/g) contradicts claims of no pore blockage. Detailed pore size distribution analysis and discussion of how such a decrease occurs without pore blocking are required.

### 11. Porosity Creation Evidence (p.9)

**Issue:** N2 physisorption evidence?

**Comment:** Claims about porosity creation in Fe2O3 shell after 900°C treatment lack supporting N2 physisorption data. Pore size distribution analysis is needed to validate these structural claims.

### 12. Particle Size Analysis (p.9)

**Issue:** Suggest adding the particle size distribution and compare it with before and after ALD deposition and DRM reaction.

**Comment:** While average particle size is reported (5 nm), complete particle size distributions comparing fresh, ALD-coated, and post-reaction catalysts would provide valuable insights into sintering resistance and structural stability.

### 13. Conformal Layer Evidence (p.9)

**Issue:** From TEM, I can see well-dispersed or deposited Fe on the catalyst, but cannot say it has a conformal Fe2O3 layer deposition.

**Comment:** The TEM images show Fe dispersion but do not conclusively demonstrate conformal layer formation. More definitive microscopic evidence or alternative characterization techniques are needed to support conformal coating claims.

### 14. Oxygen Availability Mechanism (p.10)

**Issue:** Fe2O3 ALD layer may restrict access to surface PdO, but how it enhances the reducibility and results in 300 µmol/g. Can it be ascribed to the reduction of Fe2O3, not purely by PdO reduction? The authors are required to compare it with a benchmark catalyst of Fe2O3/Al2O3.

**Comment:** The claimed oxygen availability (300 μmol/g) and enhanced reducibility are contradictory if the ALD layer restricts PdO access. A benchmark Fe2O3/Al2O3 catalyst is essential to distinguish between PdO and Fe2O3 contributions to the observed effects.

### 15. Reaction Severity Assessment (p.11)

**Issue:** May or may not be true. The severe conditions for DRM can be considered high concentration of both methane and CO2 or lower temperature than 700°C due to coke formation by both Boudouard reaction and methane decomposition.

**Comment:** The characterization of CH4-rich conditions as "severe" is questionable. Severe DRM conditions typically involve high concentrations of both reactants or lower temperatures where coking is more problematic. The authors should better justify their condition selection.

### 16. Light-off Profile Analysis (p.11)

**Issue:** To comprehensively look at the light-off DRM, it is required at least the conversion of methane and CO2. Along with my comments in the experiments, it is concerned how the reaction has been quantitatively analyzed in the absence of quantifying the internal standard (or overlapped CO and N2).

**Comment:** DRM light-off analysis requires both CH4 and CO2 conversion data. The quantitative reliability is compromised without internal standards, especially considering potential CO/N2 spectral overlap in MS analysis.

### 17. Activation Energy Discussion (p.11)

**Issue:** The apparent activation energies would give the intrinsic temperature dependence of the catalyst for DRM. The authors would be encouraged to further discuss how Pd/Al2O3 with IWI Fe2O3 and ALD Fe2O3 can change the temperature dependence compared to Pd/Al2O3 and Fe2O3/Al2O3, which would make the manuscript more insightful.

**Comment:** While activation energies are reported, the discussion lacks depth regarding how different Fe2O3 deposition methods fundamentally alter DRM kinetics. Comparison with Fe2O3/Al2O3 benchmark would provide mechanistic insights into the role of Fe2O3 location and morphology.

### 18. XPS Analysis Limitations (p.12)

**Issue:** How much of the penetration depth of XPS, not enough to go into or through the coating of coke?

**Comment:** XPS analysis of coked catalysts has limited penetration depth (~10 nm). Changes in Fe signals may not reliably indicate Fe2O3 acting as a "sacrificial layer" if coke deposits exceed XPS sampling depth. Alternative bulk characterization methods are needed to support these conclusions.

### Recommendation

This manuscript requires major revisions addressing fundamental experimental design flaws before it can be considered suitable for publication in ACS Catalysis. The current work falls short of the rigorous experimental standards expected for this premier catalysis journal.
