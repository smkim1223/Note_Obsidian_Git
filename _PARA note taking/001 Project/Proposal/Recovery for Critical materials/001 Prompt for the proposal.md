# Research Proposal: Recovery of Critical Materials (Ni, Pt, Ir, Co, Li, Mg) via Electrowinning and Chemical Precipitation

## Executive Summary

This research proposal outlines a comprehensive study to develop and optimize integrated hydrometallurgical processes for recovering critical materials from mixed waste streams. The focus is on electrowinning for Ni, Pt, Ir, and Co, and chemical precipitation for Li and Mg, targeting â‰¥99.9% metal purity. The work addresses urgent needs in sustainable resource recovery from EV battery black mass and spent catalysts, supporting circular economy principles and reducing dependency on primary mining.

## 1. Introduction and Background

### 1.1 Problem Statement

Critical materials including nickel, platinum, iridium, cobalt, lithium, and magnesium are essential for modern technologies including batteries, catalysts, and electronics. Traditional mining faces increasing environmental and economic constraints, making recovery from waste streams increasingly vital. Current hydrometallurgical processes often lack selectivity and efficiency for multi-metal systems, particularly when targeting high-purity products from complex matrices.

### 1.2 Research Significance

This research addresses key challenges in sustainable metal recovery by:
- Developing selective electrowinning processes for transition and precious metals
- Optimizing chemical precipitation routes for alkali and alkaline earth metals
- Creating integrated flowsheets that maximize recovery while minimizing waste
- Establishing lab-scale validation protocols for industrial scale-up

## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 Current State of Electrowinning Technology

Electrowinning has proven effective for metal recovery with current densities typically ranging from 500-2000 A/mÂ² for base metals and lower densities (~600 A/mÂ²) for precious metals. Recent studies on indium electrowinning using titanium cathodes achieved 84% current efficiency and 2.5 kWh/kg specific energy consumption, demonstrating the potential for optimization of operating parameters including current density, temperature, pH, and electrolyte composition.

### 2.2 Chemical Precipitation of Li and Mg

Lithium recovery via carbonate precipitation remains the industry standard, with recent advances in solvent extraction-assisted precipitation showing >99.5% magnesium removal with <10% lithium co-extraction. Binary extractant systems using Aliquat 336 and Versatic Acid 10 have demonstrated sustainable separation without consuming acids or bases.

### 2.3 Research Gaps

Current literature reveals gaps in:
- Integrated multi-metal recovery from complex matrices
- Optimization of electrowinning parameters for precious metals in presence of base metals
- Sustainable precipitation processes minimizing reagent consumption
- Process integration strategies for maximizing overall recovery efficiency

**Pretreatment Studies**:
- Selective precipitation of interfering metals (Fe, Al, Cu, Mn) using diammonium phosphate (DAP)
- pH optimization for impurity removal while retaining target metals
- Kinetic studies of precipitation reactions
